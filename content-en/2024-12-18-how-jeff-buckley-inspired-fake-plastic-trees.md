title: "How Jeff Buckley inspired Fake Plastic Trees"
date: 2024-12-18

---

“…She looks like the real thing
She tastes like the real thing
My fake plastic love...”

Radiohead, one of the famous alternative rock bands of the nineties, will be familiar to many for their all-time hit single ‘Creep’, from the album ‘Pablo Honey’ (1993).

After the overwhelming success of ‘Creep’, Radiohead came under enormous pressure while recording their second album, ‘The Bends’.

During the recording of ‘Fake Plastic Trees’, one of the guitarists, Ed O’Brien, said: “The early takes of Fake Plastic Trees were like November Rain by Guns N’ Roses. It was awful.”

One evening they decided to go and see Jeff Buckley at a live music club in London — a show for the album ‘Grace’ (1994). That show made an enormous impression on Radiohead, and on Thom Yorke in particular, who said: “In the early days when I sang, I thought I had to use a loud voice, put a lot of power into it. But after watching Jeff Buckley, he gave me the courage to sing in falsetto.”

It should be added that the head register can be reached in two ways. The first is head voice, where the upper register is powerful and the singer feels the vibration around the head. The second is falsetto, where the upper register is thinner and softer, and the singer feels the vibration around the throat.

The bassist Colin Greenwood said in an interview: “After we came back from Jeff’s show, we recorded Fake Plastic Trees again. Thom sat down and played it as an acoustic version, and by the third take, when he finished playing, he burst into tears. In the end that was the take we used.”

Finally, in 1995, Radiohead released ‘Fake Plastic Trees’ on the album ‘The Bends’, and put paid to the claim that they were a band with only one hit in ‘Creep’.

But Jeff Buckley later died in a drowning accident in 1997, and so it turned out that ‘Grace’ was the only album released before his death. It became his signature record — with a voice soft yet powerful, a voice that hypnotises the listener into sinking down into the sorrow of the story in the song. Jeff was a singer with a voice entirely his own, and he remains an inspiration to Thom Yorke and to countless other singers around the world.
