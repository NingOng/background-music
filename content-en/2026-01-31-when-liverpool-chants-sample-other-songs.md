I never imagined writing an article with both Pink Floyd and Penguin Villa in it, and yet here we are — because the two bands turn out to have one thing in common: both put the sound of Liverpool supporters into their songs.

Every club in football has a chant. It might be there to lift the team or to wind up the opposition, but the purpose has always been the same: pull the crowd together and put something behind the players.

Of all of them, one of the most famous is Liverpool’s “You’ll Never Walk Alone.”

The song started life in 1945, in the Broadway musical ‘Carousel’.

The turn came in 1963, when a local Liverpool band, Gerry and the Pacemakers, covered it — and took it to number one in the UK for four straight weeks. Not long after, Liverpool fans started singing it at their players. The club began playing it so the crowd could sing before kick-off and after the whistle, and it hardened into the club anthem it still is.

Then in 1971 Pink Floyd released ‘Meddle’, and on it is one song with the sound of Liverpool supporters threaded through it: “Fearless.”

‘Fearless’ is about taking the road that is not smooth, climbing the steep hill — however hard the going, do not be afraid, and smile at the crowd. The guitar climbs the notes as though working its way up the mountain, and at the top the cymbals come in.

Putting the crowd singing “You’ll Never Walk Alone” underneath was presumably meant to push the lyric further. Take the line:

“Fearlessly, the idiot faced the crowd, smiling.”

It is about having no fear in the middle of a crowd — the fool who smiles and takes things as they are anyway. English football singing fits that idea rather neatly.

The funny part is that three members of Pink Floyd — Roger Waters, David Gilmour, and Nick Mason — were all Arsenal supporters, from London.

Presumably the Liverpool chant went in because it fit the song, rather than for anything to do with who they supported.

Cut to Thailand, now, where one man is putting together a new single: Jay Jetamon Malayotha, otherwise known as Penguin Villa.

“ลำพัง” (Alone) was a Penguin Villa single from 2018, later included on ‘J’, the band’s second album, released the same year.

The Liverpool supporters are in the song’s intro, sitting behind everything as a background note — the same note the guitar lead is playing. Listen closely and you can just make it out.

Jay has said the idea behind ‘ลำพัง’ was a picture in his head of a place full of people where he nonetheless felt piercingly lonely. Why should somewhere like that still feel like this?

Which is when it occurred to him that the sound of a stadium crowd might carry exactly that. So he went hunting through recordings of supporters until he came across a crowd singing You’ll Never Walk Alone.

He picked out the passage where they hold a single long note, and sampled it into the intro of ‘ลำพัง’, which is what you can hear.

The other funny part is that Jay, at university, supported Manchester United — Liverpool’s oldest enemy. Which is to say: nobody in this entire article actually supports Liverpool.
