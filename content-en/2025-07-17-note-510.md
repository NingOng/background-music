คำบางคำ ; Sqweez Animal ; 2011

It is nearly the tenth anniversary of the death of Singh Musikapong, guitarist of the band Sqweez Animal — and it happens that I have only just begun listening to their music properly.

So this post is meant as a dedication to Sqweez Animal, through one song. I spent a long time choosing which one to write about, but in the end I came back around to the one I have always loved most: “คำบางคำ” (Some Words).

คำบางคำ is a song from their second album — or, to put it another way, their final album, “ไม่มีที่มา” (2011).

Win, the lead singer, has described how he and Singh worked as a kind of back-and-forth. Win would lay out the structure, the lyrics, the melody, and then Singh would take those ideas away, sit with them alone and let them drift, and send them back. On it went until the song was finished.

"Most of the time the two of us didn't run out of steam at the same moment. If one of us was flagging, the other would push a little, hold the other one up. Doing it alone might have been rough," Win said.

It was in the interviews promoting this second album that Singh described their genre as 'psychedelic soul' — that is, music with groove in its rhythm while at the same time letting the listener drift along with the sweet, hazy sound of the song.

One thing that makes คำบางคำ special is that it is one of the songs produced with the help of Tahiti 80, the French indie-pop band.

But the most special thing is surely the devotion Singh had for this song. คำบางคำ was a song Singh loved dearly. He wanted to write it himself, and even wanted to sing it himself, because he loved the words and the whole shape of it so much.

" ฉันคิดถึงเธออีกแล้ว และยิ่งมากขึ้นทุกที
พึ่งรู้ว่ารักเธอขนาดนี้ พึ่งรู้ว่าทั้งหมดใจที่มี "

" คงจะมีบางคำที่ฉันพึ่งบอกเธอไป แล้วเธอโกรธฉัน
คงต้องมีบางวันที่รักเราถูกลืมไป ช่วยยกโทษให้ฉันที "

"Singh had one of the most distinctive guitar voices of anyone. His ears heard things that I and everyone I've known didn't hear that way," from an interview with Karn of The Parkinson.

I believe Sqweez Animal's music remains an inspiration to no small number of Thai bands of the generations that followed — whether from Win's high but warm voice, or Singh's sweet, dreamlike guitar.

Ten years on, however long Singh has been gone from us, however far away he has gone, the sound of his guitar will still be ringing after the song ends, every time we put it on.
