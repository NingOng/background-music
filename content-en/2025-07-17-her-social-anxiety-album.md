Her Social Anxiety (album) ; Solitude Is Bliss ; 2015

What are you supposed to do while you are anxious?

Things happen to us often enough, and when they pass we tend to come away carrying something that cannot be got rid of, even once the thing itself is over.

This album seems to walk us through the journey of one woman — through love, desire, dreams, and the search inside her own self.

What I want to do here is talk about the songs I love, and point you toward them.

Venting (tracks 1–2):

Every time we are drunk, the picture of the real world blurs while memory only sharpens. And when the racing mind arrives along with the alcohol, music turns out to be the only way out.

Listening to ‘ระบายกับเสียงเพรียก’, it is impossible not to think of ‘Shine On You Crazy Diamond’ — the way the instrumental and vocal sections are laid out, the length of the thing, the familiar synth over the top. It should have no trouble holding on to anyone who loves progressive rock.

Desire (tracks 3–5):

To me this stretch is the heaviest on the record, whether through ‘สตรี’ or through the longing in ‘Sleep Tight (03.00 A.M.)’.

“She left everything, she gave up everything, for you”

‘สตรี’ is about a woman and a young man — a man so far gone into the desire around him that he forgets the one who loves him gave up her own dreams exactly as he did. Another song whose meaning is perfectly plain and still cuts deep.

“A scent goes past, along the path of the nostrils, and wakes me into dying out of what is real”

‘Sleep Tight (03.00 A.M.)’ takes us to the line between dream and waking, memory and delirium. A familiar smell on its own, or a scrap of an image, is enough to make us want back something that might once have been ours — as though it were sitting right there at our feet.

Selfhood (tracks 6–11):

“She was born; how many years have gone; what is it that’s good?”

‘Wanderer Land’ is a wanderer’s song: someone thinking, wondering, losing his way inside his own actions. If you are the drifting sort, waiting on fate to turn up, the thing that never comes back may be the time you spent waiting.

It suits anyone lost in their life to a strange degree, and I have to believe it pushes its listeners along at least a little.

There is no getting around it: this is one of the albums that says the most about looking for yourself — the confusion, the changing, the restless head, and the freedom. All of it laid out a piece at a time, a road toward a heart that is hard to reach the bottom of.

‘ใหม่ซ้ำเดิม’ may be my favourite thing on the record. No showy effects, nothing to nod along to, but the writing is good enough to sit with your own life for a while.

Loving something, letting something go — the shape of one’s life right now feels oddly familiar somehow. Look at it properly and a great many things are new all over again, waiting for us to go on learning them.

That is everything I have on this album. And there is really no arguing that Solitude Is Bliss are one of the bands who put words together most beautifully — in what they choose to say and in what they choose to tell us about, taking us further out into a world of music that does not end.
