title: "Haruki Murakami with The Beatles"

Most people have come across the Japanese novelist and short-story writer Haruki Murakami one way or another, and his famous book ‘Norwegian Wood’ (1987).

Anyone who has read it knows the title comes from ‘Norwegian Wood’, on The Beatles’ album ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965).

And Murakami keeps reaching for music throughout his work — not only The Beatles, but jazz and classical too.

In this book there is a whole page where the character Reiko plays guitar and sings to the narrator: fifteen-odd Beatles songs, one after another.

Here is what Murakami has said about what The Beatles did to him.

“To be honest, I wasn’t especially interested in The Beatles as a teenager. Of course I heard them constantly, because they were always on the radio. But I never once bought a record of theirs back then. I thought, it’s just fashionable stuff, something for girls. I was fairly arrogant at the time.”

“I remember hearing ‘Yesterday’ and thinking, oh no, ‘Yesterday’ again, how dull. If you heard it every single day the way I did, you’d understand.”

“But there was one time — I remember it clearly — when I was still in high school and heard ‘Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band’ (1967) for the first time. Even not being a fan, I had to admit it really was a good album.”

“The way each song was tied to the others by something — what we call a concept album — astonished me. As it turns out I still remember every song on it to this day. When I got older and listened again, I found out how special it was. I think it comes down to the gap in time, or in age, between the person sending the message and the person receiving it.”

“I never really listened to The Beatles properly until I was forty. I started paying attention while travelling in Greece. I put on a cassette, and that was when I felt something, somewhere deep down. That was when I began writing ‘Norwegian Wood’.”

“‘Penny Lane’ and ‘Strawberry Fields Forever’ astonished me. They really were astonishing the first time. But my favourite would have to be ‘The Fool on the Hill’.”

References to The Beatles turn up all over Murakami’s books, sometimes stated outright, sometimes slipped in through a title. Here are the ones hiding in the titles of his novels and stories.

The novel “Norwegian Wood” (1987) — a song on ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965)
The story “Honey Pie,” from ‘After the Quake’ (2000) — a song on ‘The Beatles’ (1968)
The story “Drive My Car,” from ‘Men Without Women’ (2014) — the opening track of ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965)
The story “Yesterday,” from ‘Men Without Women’ (2014) — a song on ‘Help!’ (1965)
The story “With the Beatles,” from ‘First Person Singular’ (2020)

The Beatles clearly did something enormous to the music industry worldwide, and Japan was no exception. The 1960s in Japan were politically tense, still working through the aftermath of the Second World War, and students at university after university took to the streets. It was Beatles songs that served as the background music for that generation — Haruki Murakami included.
