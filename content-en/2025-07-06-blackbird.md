“Blackbird” is a song from the ninth studio album by The Beatles, self-titled, better known to everyone as the ‘White Album’ (1968).

‘Blackbird’ was written by Paul McCartney, the band’s lead singer. The track features only Paul’s voice, a guitar, the tapping of a foot keeping time, and the call of a blackbird at the end of the song.

On the music, Paul has said he was inspired by the “Bourrée in E minor” by Johann Sebastian Bach, the Baroque-era classical composer.

Paul added that the Bach piece was a party trick from his younger days, something he and George Harrison, the Beatles’ guitarist, used to show off to each other.

In adapting it, Paul took Bach’s melodic movement and bass line, but reworked the chords so that they would sound contemporary and reach the emotions more directly.

As for the lyrics, Paul drew on two things.

The first was the call of the blackbird, which came from hearing the bird sing in the morning while he and his bandmates were staying in Rishikesh, India.

The second was that this was a period when the United States was seething with tension over African-American civil rights. Paul wrote the lyrics drawing inspiration from the ‘Little Rock Nine’ — the episode in which nine Black students were blocked from attending a high school in the state of Arkansas.

Although by law the students had the right to be educated at the school, within Arkansas the students, the parents, and even the state governor blocked Black students from studying alongside white ones. But those students still stood firm in exercising the basic constitutional right of everyone to an equal education, until at last they were able to attend.

And even after they were admitted, the Little Rock Nine were bullied inside the school. Their story became a subject of wide debate about civil rights in America. The episode was an important starting point for the Black civil rights movement, and a declaration that they would not let anyone take their basic rights from them.

That is why the ‘Blackbird’ in the song is not merely a little black bird with a broken wing trying to fly, but also a symbol for those young students who, however powerless, however blinded by the harm done to them, still stood firm and fought for their own dreams.
