When Liverpool chants sample other songs

31 January 2026

I never dreamed I would write an article with both Pink Floyd and Penguin Villa in it, but today it has happened — because these two bands turn out to share one thing in common: they both put the singing of Liverpool supporters into their songs.

When it comes to chants in the world of football, every team has one. They may be there to lift spirits or even to mock the opposition, but the purpose has always been the same: to unite the fans and to give their players a powerful lift.

Among all the football teams out there, one of the most famous chants of all is “You’ll Never Walk Alone,” belonging to Liverpool in England.

“You’ll Never Walk Alone” began life in 1945 as a song from the Broadway musical ‘Carousel’ in the United States.

The turning point came in 1963, when a local Liverpool band, Gerry and the Pacemakers, covered the song from that stage musical — and it climbed to number one in the UK charts for four consecutive weeks. Not long after, Liverpool fans began singing it as a chant for their players. The club started playing it so that the crowd could sing before kick-off and after the final whistle. The song became an institution, the legendary club anthem of Liverpool to this day.

Later, in 1971, Pink Floyd, the English rock band, released the album ‘Meddle’, which contains one song with the singing of Liverpool supporters woven into it: “Fearless.”

‘Fearless’ speaks of choosing the road that is not smooth, of climbing a steep mountain — however hard the path, do not be afraid, and smile at the crowd. Along with it, the guitar climbs the notes as though slowly scaling the mountain, until, at the summit, the cymbals ring out.

Pink Floyd’s decision to bring in the sound of the crowd singing “You’ll Never Walk Alone” was presumably meant to reinforce the meaning of the lyrics. Take, for instance, the line:

“Fearlessly, the idiot faced the crowd, smiling.”

It speaks of being without fear in the midst of a crowd — of a fool who still smiles and accepts things as they are. The context of English football singing fits the idea of the lyrics rather well.

The funny thing about all this is that three members of Pink Floyd — Roger Waters, David Gilmour, and Nick Mason — were all Arsenal supporters, from London.

The reason for putting a Liverpool song in was presumably that they wanted a chant that fit the context of the song, rather than anything to do with the team they supported.

Cut to present-day Thailand, where one man is crafting a new single: Jay Jetamon Malayotha, or Penguin Villa.

“Lamphang” (Alone) was a Penguin Villa single from 2018, later included on ‘J’, the band’s second album, released the same year.

The part with the Liverpool supporters is in the song’s intro, as a background note behind everything — the same note as the guitar lead. Listen closely and you can just make it out.

Jay himself has said that the concept of ‘Lamphang’ was a picture in his mind of a place full of people where, even so, he felt piercingly lonely. Why is it that in a place like this you can still feel this way?

That was when the idea struck him: the sound of a crowd in a stadium might convey exactly that. So he went hunting for recordings of supporters of various clubs, until he came across a chant of “You’ll Never Walk Alone.”

He then picked the passage where the crowd holds a single long note, and sampled it into the intro of ‘Lamphang’, as you can hear.

Another funny thing is that Jay, back in his university days, supported Manchester United — Liverpool’s eternal rivals. So to sum up everything in this article: not one person in it actually supports Liverpool.
