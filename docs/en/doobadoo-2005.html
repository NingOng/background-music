THEN

Artist

DOObaDOO ; 2005

10 September 2026

“…Dear listener, close your eyes and listen to this song…”

Dear reader, I’d like you to give this piece a try, because the words and the music just might warm your heart.

DOObaDOO is a jazz-pop duo formed by two people: the producer Oh Sepia and the singer Lukwa Pijika.

Oh had started out with Sepia, an alternative rock band of the nineties. Then he got the chance to work with a singer from CU Band, the band from Chulalongkorn University, and that singer was Lukwa.

That collaboration produced the song that started it all for the two of them, before DOObaDOO even existed: Sepia’s “อยากอยู่กับเธอทั้งคืน” (I Want to Stay With You All Night), from the album “ไม่ต้องใส่ถุง” (No Bag Needed).

When they formed the band, the name came from the sound jazz musicians make when they hum a tune: doo-ba-doo. It suited the kind of music they meant to make, and it was easy to say and hard to forget.

“เก็บกระเป๋า” (Packing My Bag), the album’s opening track, began as homework. Oh told Lukwa to write anything at all and bring it to him to read. She wrote a diary, he set it to music, and that became the song.

The next track, “ไม่ใช่ผู้ชาย” (I’m Not a Man), is probably DOObaDOO’s catchiest. It came out of a conversation Oh had with his friend, the comedian Note Udom, who told him: “Women want to tell a man they love him, but they don’t dare. They can’t. Make a song about that and you’ll have a huge following of women.”

Oh didn’t think much of it at the time. But some while later, swimming laps in the 25-metre pool on the roof of his condo, he says a melody slowly started singing itself in his head, and then a drum beat came in behind it. As soon as he got out of the pool he rushed to get the song down in his music software.

A few days later he called Lukwa in to record. A few takes later, “I’m Not a Man” was finished.

Beyond those two hits, DOObaDOO have plenty of other lovely, sweet songs. I’d encourage you to try “สุขุมวิท” (Sukhumvit), “คุณผู้ฟังที่รัก (หลังไมค์)” (Dear Listener, Off Air), “คุณนายมะขาม” (Mrs. Tamarind), “สวย” (Beautiful) and “เมื่อคืนนี้ฉันฝันถึงคุณ” (I Dreamed of You Last Night).

Oh’s jazz guitar, the playful lyrics and Lukwa’s soft, sweet voice add up to music that is gentle and very good at soothing a heart.

“…Dear listener, when the song is over, please call me for a little while. I never meant to bother you, never thought of it. I only wanted to tell you I love you through a song…”
