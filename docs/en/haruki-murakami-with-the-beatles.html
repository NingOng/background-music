Haruki Murakami with The Beatles

8 December 2024

Most people have probably come across the celebrated Japanese novelist and short-story writer Haruki Murakami, and his famous book ‘Norwegian Wood’ (1987).

Anyone who has read it knows that the title comes from the song ‘Norwegian Wood’ on The Beatles’ album ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965).

And time and again we find Murakami referring to music throughout his writing — not only The Beatles, but jazz and classical music as well.

In this book there is a full page in which the character Reiko plays guitar and sings to the protagonist — more than fifteen Beatles songs in a row.

Let us look at what Murakami has said about The Beatles’ influence on him.

“To be honest, I wasn’t particularly interested in The Beatles as a teenager. Of course I heard their songs all the time, because they were always on the radio. But I never bought a single one of their records back then. I thought, ‘it’s just fashionable music, something for girls to listen to.’ I was rather arrogant at the time.”

“I remember hearing ‘Yesterday’ and thinking, ‘oh no, ‘Yesterday’ again, how boring.’ If you heard it every single day like that, you’d understand me.”

“But there was one time — I remember it well — when I was still in high school and heard the album ‘Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band’ (1967) for the first time. Even though I wasn’t a Beatles fan, I had to admit it really was a good album.”

“The way each song was connected to the others by something — what we call a ‘concept album’ — amazed me. As it turns out, I still remember every song on that album to this day. When I got older and listened again, I found how special an album it was. I think it comes down to the difference in time, or in age, between the one sending the message and the one receiving it.”

“I never really listened to The Beatles attentively until I was forty. I started listening properly while travelling in Greece. I put on a cassette tape, and that was when I felt something, deep inside. That was when I began writing ‘Norwegian Wood’.”

“I was quite astonished by ‘Penny Lane’ and ‘Strawberry Fields Forever’. They really were astonishing the first time I heard them. But my favourite would have to be ‘The Fool on the Hill’.”

There are many places where we find references to The Beatles in Murakami’s books, whether stated outright or slipped in through a title. What follows are the references to The Beatles in the titles of his novels and short stories.

The novel “Norwegian Wood” (1987), a song on the album ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965)
The short story “Honey Pie” from the collection ‘After the Quake’ (2000); Honey Pie is a song on the album ‘The Beatles’ (1968)
The short story “Drive My Car” from the collection ‘Men Without Women’ (2014); Drive My Car is the opening track on ‘Rubber Soul’ (1965)
The short story “Yesterday” from the collection ‘Men Without Women’ (2014); Yesterday is a song on the album ‘Help!’ (1965)
The short story “With the Beatles” from the collection ‘First Person Singular’ (2020)

It is clear that The Beatles had an enormous effect on the music industry worldwide, and Japan was no exception. The 1960s were a time of political tension in Japan in the aftermath of the Second World War; students at many universities took to the streets in protest. And it was The Beatles’ music that served as the background music of that student generation — Haruki Murakami included.
