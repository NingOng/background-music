THEN

Song

忘記他 (Forget Him)

25 May 2025

“In a few days a woman will come asking after me. Give her this coin, and tell her 1818 is my lucky number.”

“1818 is a code on the jukebox. Once she plays it, she will understand what it means.”

“…To forget him — how is that any different from forgetting everything?
Everything gone, no direction left, my own self faded and abandoned…”

“忘記他” (Forget Him) is a lonely song that refuses to leave you alone. But long before it turned up on the soundtrack of Wong Kar-wai’s ‘Fallen Angels’,

it was written for Taiwan’s enduring legend among singers, Teresa Teng.

Teresa Teng was famous for singing in an unusual number of languages: Mandarin, Taiwanese Hokkien, Hong Kong Cantonese, and Japanese and English besides.

“忘記他” appeared on her first Cantonese album, in 1980, and one thing sets it apart: it draws on the Japanese genre of enka.

Enka means several things depending on who is using the word, but for our purposes it means a kind of ballad — slow, with a melody leaning toward sadness, aimed squarely at the listener’s feelings. It rose to popularity in the years after the Second World War.

Enka works off the pentatonic scale, and usually brings in a mandolin and bowed strings such as violins.

That enka influence is what made the song sit apart from other Hong Kong records of the period.

The other thing is Teresa Teng’s image. She came across as composed, well-mannered, forever smiling — and this is one of very few songs of hers that lets the anguish, the holding back, and the loneliness of love show through.

Fifteen years later, Shirley Kwan recorded it again as dream pop: hazy, the vocals and harmonies and instruments all sounding as though they had wandered out of somebody’s sleep.

It was Shirley’s “忘記他” that Wong Kar-wai used in ‘Fallen Angels’ (1995).

In the scene, it belongs to two killers who have worked together for years but who, for reasons of their own, can never meet. When the last job is done, she has to forget him — the two of them have to forget that the other exists — though they never met in the first place.

“Only he made me see any worth in myself, gave me permission to love.
From something ordinary into something beautiful. Forget him… how am I supposed to do that?”

Sources: "何日君再来-纪念邓丽君逝世十周年(3)_影音娱乐_新浪网”; Interpretation of Cantonese Songs: The Changing Voice of Hong Kong; "林奕華：翁倩玉的仙人仙氣"
