THEN

Album

Cafe by Moderndog

29 October 2026

“…Deep down, you already know
what it is, the thing your heart wants…”

“After the first album took off, we got scared. ‘Hey, can we write songs like that again? We started out with บุษบา (Busaba). Can it ever be as good as that?’”

That was Pod, Moderndog’s frontman, talking about the time after their debut sold more than 600,000 cassettes.

The band and their producer, Suki Kamol Sukosol Clapp, were under enormous pressure to make a second album that wouldn’t just repeat the first.

“We wrote on instinct. We weren’t professional songwriters, so we decided to go and learn how.

I flew to New York and studied there for almost two years. Studying, living. And it really did help. The songs on the second album came out of that life in New York.”

But just as the second album was getting under way, Bob, the band’s bassist at the time, decided to leave to study abroad. That left only three of them.

As luck would have it, Koh Nathapol Srichomkwan had just graduated in music from Berklee. He came home, recorded the bass for Moderndog’s second album, and toured with the band for about a year.

Koh later joined P.O.P, founded Groove Riders, and has produced a great many records since.

“คาเฟ่” (Café), Moderndog’s second album, released in 1997, rebelled against the usual rules of alternative music and, more importantly, against the band’s own first album.

The sound design and guitar effects of Methee Noijinda feel raw, at once retro and ahead of their time, and the mix is just right.

Part of what makes the songs sound so modern is the percussion, the electronic drums running through track after track.

At the same time there are sounds like gongs and other Eastern percussion in “เกือก” (Shoes), and that song even slips in a sample of the guitar riff from Busaba.

Overall the album has a rawness, and a sound design, close to Radiohead’s The Bends (1995), and to some of the songs on The Stone Roses’ Second Coming (1994).

I noticed something odd about these alternative rock bands: they all ran into the same problem. A huge debut brought fame, and then confusion about what to make next.

For Radiohead it was “Creep”. For The Stone Roses, “I Wanna Be Adored”. For Moderndog, Busaba.

Bands often cling to an old success. These three showed that they were willing to experiment, willing to risk making music in ways nobody had tried.

In the end they proved they weren’t one-hit wonders who flare up overnight and burn out. They were bands that kept growing, and had the nerve to show what they truly felt at that moment.

“After the second album, which honestly did all right, it had ติ๋ม (Tim) and some other songs, it wasn’t a flop, I still went off and became a forest monk for four months. I lived in the forest, lived a life out there, and learned about myself.”

“That was my idea of how life should go back then: after the first album, go abroad; after the second, go inward. So that’s where I went. And that’s why the third album came out as Love Me Love My Life.”

So says Pod, who is still travelling, still showing what he truly feels, even as that changes with time.

“…You probably know full well what you want, but because of something you built yourself, whatever you try, you can’t get past it. What you built is just too solid…”
