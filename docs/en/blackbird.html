Song

Blackbird

6 July 2025

“You have waited your whole life for this moment to fly.”

“A small bird sings out into the night,
its wings drained of colour, waiting on the sun.
Its wings are broken — and yet what does it ask for?
Only to take flight, just once.”

“Blackbird” comes from The Beatles’ ninth studio album, self-titled, though everyone knows it as the ‘White Album’ (1968).

It was written by Paul McCartney, and the track holds almost nothing: Paul’s voice, a guitar, a foot tapping out the time, and the call of a blackbird at the end.

Musically, Paul has said the inspiration was Bach’s “Bourrée in E minor,” from the Baroque era.

He added that the Bach piece had been a party trick in his younger days — something he and George Harrison used to play at each other to show off.

Adapting it, Paul kept Bach’s melodic movement and bass line but reworked the chords so they would sound of their own time and land harder emotionally.

The lyrics came from two places.

The first was the blackbird itself. He and the band had heard the bird calling in the mornings while they were staying in Rishikesh, India.

The second was that America at that moment was boiling over with tension around African-American civil rights. Paul wrote the words with the Little Rock Nine in mind — the nine Black students who were barred from attending a high school in Arkansas.

Legally the students had every right to be there. Inside Arkansas, though, students, parents, and the state governor himself all worked to keep Black students from sitting in the same classrooms as white ones. The nine held their ground on the basic constitutional principle that everyone is entitled to an equal education, and in the end they got through the door.

Even then, the Little Rock Nine were tormented inside the school. Their story became a national argument about civil rights in America, and it marked an important beginning for the Black civil rights movement — a statement that they would not let anyone take their most basic rights from them.

Which is why the blackbird in the song is not just a small black bird with a broken wing trying to get airborne. It is also those young students: powerless, half-blinded by what had been done to them, and still standing, still fighting their way toward what they wanted.
