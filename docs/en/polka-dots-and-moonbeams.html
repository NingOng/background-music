Polka Dots And Moonbeams

12 October 2026

“Polka Dots And Moonbeams” is a jazz standard said to be the first song that made Frank Sinatra’s name. It came out in the 1940s, as the Second World War was drawing to a close.

The polka dots of the lyric belong to a woman at a country dance: a man meets a girl there wearing a dress covered in them.

The song arrived just as polka-dot clothing was in fashion in America, a pattern people wore with a kind of national pride as the Second World War came to an end.

Listen to a sung version, Sinatra’s for instance, and you get the lines:

“Suddenly I saw polka dots and moonbeams
All around a pug-nosed dream”

I was a little startled the first time I read “pug-nosed dream”. Was the song making fun of her face? It turns out “pug-nosed” just means a nose that turns up slightly at the tip, a sign of something cute and natural.

So a “pug-nosed dream” is a sweet girl with a turned-up nose, the girl of his dreams, standing in soft moonlight.

“Polka Dots And Moonbeams” has since been sung and played many times over, by Ella Fitzgerald, Wes Montgomery, Bill Evans, Lester Young and plenty of other jazz greats.
